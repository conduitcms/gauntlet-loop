# Source notes and credit

The **Gauntlet Loop** name and the specific lead-builder-fresh-critic pattern are credited to Matt Shumer. His public explanation emphasizes an ambitious goal, a concrete comparison bar, agent-chosen decomposition, direct inspection of the artifact, separate critics with fresh context, and continued revision while meaningful gaps remain.

Primary method source:

- Matt Shumer, “How to Run a Gauntlet Loop”: https://somethingbig.ai/gauntlet-loop
- Original public prompt and project: https://github.com/mshumer/Claude-of-Duty/blob/main/prompt.md

The broader loop-engineering guide from The Prompt Index develops the objective-metric-boundary frame, durable state, permission limits, observable evidence, failure recovery, diminishing-return stops, and examples across domains:

- “AI Loop Engineering in 2026: How to Build a Gauntlet Loop”: https://www.thepromptindex.com/ai-loop-engineering-gauntlet-loop-guide.html

This skill is an original, cross-domain operational adaptation. It intentionally replaces unbounded “until perfect” language with explicit success, cost, risk, permission, observability, repeated-failure, and human-escalation boundaries. It also adapts the method for environments that do not support genuine subagent independence and requires those limitations to be disclosed.
